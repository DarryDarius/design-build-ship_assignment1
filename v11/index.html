0°F air plus a 15 mph lake wind feels like −19°F on bare skin. At that wind chill, frostbite can set in within about 30 minutes. Always check the “feels like” number.
The Layering System

Three thin layers beat one big coat.

pp. 02–03
Fig. 1 · Exploded view, inside → out
01

Base layer next to skin

A thin, snug top and leggings that pull sweat away from your skin so it can't chill you. Never cotton: it soaks up moisture and stays wet.

Material
Merino wool or synthetic
Get it cheap
Uniqlo HEATTECH, Costco multipacks
02

Mid layer the warmth

Fleece or a light down jacket. It holds a pocket of warm air around you. Unzip or take it off in an overheated lecture hall.

Material
Fleece or light down
Get it cheap
Thrift stores, the November gear swap
03

Shell the weather

A windproof, waterproof parka with a hood that reaches past your hips. This is what stops the lake wind.

Material
Windproof, waterproof outer
Fit
Past the hips, room for layers underneath
System note: three layers trap more air than one heavy coat, and you can adjust them. Cold outside, hot inside: take off a layer instead of sweating through your only one.

Club guidance, not a guarantee. Ratings assume you are moving, dry, and outside for a normal walk to class. If skin feels numb, stings, or turns pale, go inside and warm up gradually. Frostbite times are for exposed skin.

Field Notes · Tested by

“I lasted eight minutes at the bus stop.”

Ama, second-year, from Accra. It never snows there.

My first January I wore my one cotton hoodie under a fashion coat and thought I was ready. The air said 12°F. The wind chill said −10°F. My ears hurt for an hour.

Care Instructions

For the wearer, not the clothes. The dark is part of the cold.

Get midday light

On Dec 21 the sun sets around 4:20 PM. Step outside at lunch, even when it's cold.

Vitamin D

Less sun means less vitamin D. Ask a doctor whether you should take it.

Check in on friends

February is long. If someone goes quiet, knock on their door.

Thursdays, 6 PM

The club meets on campus every week. Come cold, leave warmer.

The Gear Swap.

November. Free. On campus.

Last year's first-years became this year's second-years, and their closets are full of parkas, boots, and mittens they've outgrown or replaced. Once a year, right before the real cold, we put it all on racks and you take what fits. No money changes hands.

When
November, before the deep cold. Date sent to members.
Where
On campus. Room sent to members.
Bring
Clean coats, boots, hats, mittens, fleece. Optional.
Take
Whatever fits and keeps you warm.
