THE THESES.

EIGHT RULES. NO EXCEPTIONS. WRITTEN BY STUDENTS WHO WERE COLD SO YOU DO NOT HAVE TO BE.

I.

COTTON
IS A LIE.

Cotton holds sweat. Wet fabric pulls heat out of your body. Your favorite hoodie is a cold compress. Base layer: merino or synthetic. Never cotton.

II.

THE TEMPERATURE IS NOT THE NUMBER. THE WIND CHILL IS.

The lake wind strips heat off your skin. 0°F with a 15 mph wind feels like −19°F. −10°F with a 20 mph wind feels like −35°F. Set your weather app to show wind chill. Read it before you open the door.

III.

THREE LAYERS.
ONE BIG COAT.

Air between layers is the insulation. One thick coat traps less of it, and you cannot take half of it off when you walk into a hot classroom. Three layers. Every day. December to March.

IV.

COVER
EVERYTHING.

Frostbite starts where you forgot. Ears. Fingers. Nose. Toes. No bare skin when the wind chill drops below zero.

VI.

THE DARK IS PART OF THE COLD.

On December 21 the sun sets around 4:20 PM. You get about nine hours of daylight. It gets into your head. Go outside at midday. Ask a doctor about vitamin D. Notice when a friend goes quiet.

VII.

NOBODY DOES THEIR FIRST WINTER ALONE.

We meet every Thursday at 6 PM on campus. Someone who survived last winter. Someone from as far away as you. Hot drinks. Bad jokes about the wind. Check in on each other.

VIII.

PREPARE IN OCTOBER.
NOT IN JANUARY.

The first snow usually arrives in November. The real cold follows. Gear up while the stores are stocked and the sidewalks are dry. Start now.
