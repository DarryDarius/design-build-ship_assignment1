WINTER(1)                  First Winter Club Manual                  WINTER(1)

NAME
       winter — survive your first Chicago winter

SYNOPSIS
       winter --survive [--first-time] [--layers=3] [--no-cotton]
              [--check-windchill] [--cover=hat,mittens,gaiter]
              [--boots=insulated,waterproof,grip] [--budget] [--with-friends]

DESCRIPTION
       Chicago winter is survivable if you prepare. The first snow usually
       arrives in November. In January the average high is 31°F and the
       average low is 18°F, but the number that decides how cold you feel is
       the wind chill: the wind strips warm air off exposed skin. Wind chills
       below 0°F are routine; -30°F or lower happens. In the January 2019
       polar vortex the air hit -23°F and the wind chill was near -50°F.

OPTIONS
       --layers=3
              Three thin layers trap warm air and come off indoors. One giant
              coat does not.
                base   thermal top + leggings. merino or synthetic.
                mid    fleece or light down. holds the heat.
                shell  windproof, waterproof parka. covers the hips.

       --no-cotton
              Cotton holds sweat and moisture and goes cold. Applies to socks.

       --check-windchill
              Read the wind chill before you leave. Frostbite on exposed skin:
                -18°F  in about 30 minutes
                -35°F  in about 10 minutes
                -48°F  in about 5 minutes

       --cover=hat,mittens,gaiter
              Hat over the ears. Mittens beat gloves. Scarf or neck gaiter
              over nose and mouth.

       --boots=insulated,waterproof,grip
              Sidewalks stay icy for weeks. Grip is not optional.

       --budget
              Thrift stores, Uniqlo HEATTECH, Costco, and the club's November
              gear swap. Spend on the shell and boots; save on the rest.

       --with-friends
              First Winter Club meets Thursdays at 18:00 on campus. Members
              get the free First Winter Checklist. Check in on each other.

ENVIRONMENT
       DAYLIGHT=9h08m
              On Dec 21. Get light in the middle of the day. Ask a doctor
              whether vitamin D makes sense for you.

BUGS
       Sunset at 16:20. Known issue; no fix scheduled before March.
       Workaround: eat lunch near a window, or outside.

       Wind off the lake ignores --layers=1.

       A cotton hoodie will report itself as a coat. It is not.

SEE ALSO
       join(1), windchill(1), layers(7), gear-swap(8)

# FIRST WINTER CHECKLIST  (preview · full version free for members)

## before the first snow (november)
[ ] base layer: thermal top + leggings, merino or synthetic   # never cotton
[ ] mid layer: fleece or light down
[ ] shell: windproof, waterproof parka that covers your hips
[ ] hat that covers the ears
[ ] mittens                                                    # beat gloves
[ ] scarf or neck gaiter
[ ] insulated waterproof boots with real grip

## every morning
[ ] check the WIND CHILL, not the temperature
[ ] below -18°F wind chill: cover every inch of skin

## december–february
[ ] get midday daylight (sunset is ~16:20 in december)
[ ] ask a doctor about vitamin D
[ ] check in on a friend this week
