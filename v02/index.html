Being the Journal of

The First Winter Expeditionto Hyde Park, Illinois

Departing November · Returning, God willing, in April
The 14th of November

We arrived from warmer latitudes with nothing but a cotton hoodie and misplaced confidence. The locals speak of something called "wind chill" in low voices. Today the thermometer read 28 degrees, yet the wind off the lake made it feel near 15. I could not feel my ears by the time I reached the quadrangle.

Resolved: to procure a proper hat, one that covers the ears entirely.

Fig. 1. A rough chart of the territory, drawn with frozen fingers.
The 3rd of December

The sun now sets at twenty past four. A fellow traveller from Singapore showed me the secret: three layers, not one heavy coat. A thin thermal against the skin, a fleece above it, and a windproof shell over all. I was warm for the first time in a week.

She is a member of a society called the First Winter Club. They meet on Thursday evenings, trade boots, and drink a great deal of tea.

Wind chill of −18°F: frostbite in thirty minutes. Of −35°F: in ten. Check the "feels like," never the temperature!

The 30th of January

Minus twenty-two with the wind. The campus was silent. I was not afraid. I knew which buildings to cut through, I wore my mittens, and I had friends waiting with tea. We shall all see April.
