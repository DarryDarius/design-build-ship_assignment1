The final choice · v25

First Winter Club: cold facts, warm people.

A landing page for UChicago international students facing their first Chicago winter, especially those who've never seen snow. It should leave them thinking "this is survivable, and I'm not doing it alone," and then they should join the club and get the free First Winter Checklist.

Twelve directions went wide, five rounds narrowed them, and this is where it landed: a senior student's letter on one side, a practical gear catalog on the other, set on a winter night where the only warm light comes from people.

Step 01

Pick an idea

ChosenFirst Winter ClubA peer group that helps international students survive their first Chicago winter.
DroppedHyde Park Dumpling Supper ClubWarm, with a clear action (book a seat), but food pages are a crowded topic.
DroppedSunrise Run ClubClear action, but a narrow visual range, and nobody runs by the lake in January.

Why this one: it's the least likely to collide with 700 other pages, "cold" opens a huge visual range (a weather alert, a diary, a manual, a newspaper), and the visitor is very specific: someone from a warm city, anxious about something they've never felt.

Step 02 · v01–v12

Go wide

Twelve directions that differ in era, layout, and voice, not just color. Same visitor, same facts, same action every time. Grey cards were dropped at the narrowing step, and green ones carried forward.

Step 03

Narrow

Chosen · Plan 1Warm gear guidev11's catalog + v08's letter voice + v05/v01's wind chill tools. Reassurance and a list you can act on.
Dropped · Plan 2Winter gazettev06's newspaper + v07's brutalist headlines + v12's departures board. Striking, but it shows off, it can feel alarming, and it's dense on a phone.

What I dropped and why: GeoCities (v09) and the terminal (v10) are too noisy or too technical for this visitor. Civil defense (v04) and the manifesto (v07) are too alarming for someone already nervous. The newspaper (v06) is too dense on a phone. What I kept: the senior-student narrator from v08, the product spec cards from v11, and the wind chill math from v01/v05.

Step 04 · v13–v15

Combine

Three arrangements of the same ingredients. v15 won: company on the left, checklist on the right. v13 was easy to scan but looked like any product page, and v14 was the warmest but buried the facts in the story.

Step 05 · v16–v19

Vary

Each variation fixes or tests one thing about v15. All four ideas made it into the merge, and the cold night palette (v19) became the concept: cold facts, warm people.

Step 06 · v20–v25

Converge

v20 merges v16–v19. After that, each version refines one thing and leaves the rest alone, so every step can be compared with the one before it.

Working with Claude Code

Who did what

What I asked for

  • Three ideas, compared side by side, with a recommendation.
  • Sixteen candidate directions to choose twelve from.
  • At every fork, two or three options compared, and a recommendation.

What I decided

  • The idea, the visitor, and the one action.
  • Which 12 directions to build.
  • Plan 1 over Plan 2, v15 over v13/v14, and merging all four variations, including the night palette.

How Claude worked

  • Wrote v01–v04 directly, then ran parallel subagents for the later batches, 2–4 at a time.
  • Checked every page with headless Chrome screenshots and fixed what broke.
  • Kept to plain HTML/CSS/JS: no frameworks, no external requests, no stored data.
