First Winter Club

a letter, for your first Chicago winter

Hyde Park, the first week of October

Dear you, arriving this fall,

I don’t know your name yet, or where you’re flying in from. But if it’s somewhere like Chennai, where January means about 84°F and the warmest thing I owned was a school cardigan, then I know a little about the winter you’re about to have. Three winters ago it was mine.

So on this side of the page is what I wish someone had told me. On the other side is everything you’ll actually need, with prices. The short version: a Chicago winter is survivable if you prepare.

→ 01 · The Layering System

I made the cotton hoodie mistake, which I think everyone from somewhere warm makes exactly once. One thick hoodie under one enormous coat. I sweated on the walk to my nine o’clock, the cotton held onto every drop, and by evening I was colder than if I’d worn less.

What works is three thin things instead of one thick one. And when you walk into an overheated classroom, you can take one off.

→ 02 · The Kit

The small things matter more than I expected. A hat that comes down over your ears. Mittens, not gloves, so your fingers keep each other company. Something you can pull up over your nose.

And boots with real grip on the sole. I fell twice on the ice in my sneakers before I gave in.

→ 03 · Rated To

The first really cold morning came in early January. My phone said 2°F, and I thought, fine, I have a coat. Then the wind came off the lake, and the number that mattered was the small one underneath: feels like −20°F. My eyelashes stuck together.

In Chicago, the wind chill matters more than the temperature. I copied this onto a sticky note by my door:

feels like −18°Ffrostbite in ~30 min

feels like −35°Fabout 10 min

feels like −48°Fabout 5 min

It isn’t meant to frighten you. It’s only the number to check before you leave, the way at home you’d glance at the sky for rain.

→ 04 · The Gear Swap

You don’t need to spend much, which surprised me most. My first real parka came from a thrift store and cost less than a textbook. My base layers were Uniqlo HEATTECH. A friend with a Costco membership took a few of us along for wool socks in bulk.

And every November there’s the gear swap. Half of what I wear now once kept someone else warm.

→ 05 · Care Instructions

The part nobody mentions is the dark. On December 21 the sun sets around 4:20, and there are about nine hours of daylight. For weeks I didn’t understand why I felt so heavy. It wasn’t only homesickness. It was the light, too.

What helped: fifteen minutes outside at midday. Asking a doctor about vitamin D instead of guessing. And letting people check in on me.

THU6 PMON CAMPUS

Thursday nights are what saved me. In my first November, a girl from Lagos I’d met in a lab section asked if I wanted to come to a thing at six. It was First Winter Club: a room on campus, a kettle, a pile of mismatched mittens, and a couple of dozen people from Manila and São Paulo and Jakarta, comparing the coldest they had ever been.

Nobody made me talk if I didn’t want to. Most weeks we just sat near the radiator and were warm together, which turned out to be the whole point.

The first snow usually comes in November. When it does, please go outside and look up.

Warmly,

Meera

third year · still from Chennai · now also a little from here
