First Winter Club

a letter, for your first Chicago winter

Dear you, arriving this fall,

If you’re flying in from somewhere like Chennai, where January means 84°F, then I know a little about the winter you’re about to have. Three winters ago it was mine.

This side of the page is what I wish someone had told me. The other side is what you’ll need, with prices. The short version: a Chicago winter is survivable if you prepare.

→ 01 · Layers

I made the cotton hoodie mistake, which everyone from somewhere warm makes exactly once. One thick hoodie under one enormous coat. I sweated on the walk to my nine o’clock, the cotton stayed damp, and by evening I was colder than if I’d worn less.

Three thin things beat one thick one.

→ 02 · The Kit

The small things matter more than I expected. A hat over your ears. Mittens, so your fingers keep each other company. And boots with real grip: I fell twice on the ice in my sneakers before I gave in.

→ 03 · Rated To

My first really cold morning, my phone said 2°F. Then the wind came off the lake, and the number that mattered was the small one underneath: feels like −20°F. My eyelashes stuck together. I copied this onto a sticky note by my door:

feels like −18°Ffrostbite in ~30 min

feels like −35°Fabout 10 min

feels like −48°Fabout 5 min

It’s just the number to check before you leave, the way at home you’d glance at the sky for rain.

→ 04 · The Gear Swap

You don’t need to spend much. My first real parka came from a thrift store and cost less than a textbook. Half of what I wear now once kept someone else warm.

One more thing: by December the sun sets at 4:20. Step outside at midday, and let people check in on you.

The rest of this letter is at the bottom. ↓

THU6 PMON CAMPUS

Thursday nights are what saved me. My first November, a girl from Lagos in my lab section asked if I wanted to come to a thing at six. It was First Winter Club: a room on campus, a kettle, a pile of mismatched mittens, and people from Manila and São Paulo and Jakarta comparing the coldest they had ever been.

Nobody made me talk. Most weeks we just sat near the radiator and were warm together, which turned out to be the whole point.

Warmly,

Meera

third year · still from Chennai · now also a little from here
